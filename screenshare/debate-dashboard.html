Topic 1 · the fight

Inflation is 3.4%. The national mood is 47.8. The Fed just made your money more expensive. Were they right — or did they kick 300 million people while they were down?

Plain English: the Fed raises rates to cool prices by making borrowing hurt. It just did that for the first time in three years — on the same week consumer sentiment printed its second-lowest reading ever, gas hit $4.48, and a war is setting the price of oil. One side says inflation has been above target for years and someone finally acted. The other says you can't rate-hike a war, and the only thing this cools is you.

Topic 3

Buy-now-pay-later is invisible debt. Should that even be legal?

Plain English: “pay in four” splits a purchase into four payments, usually interest-free. For most of these plans the loan is never furnished to the credit bureaus — so paying it perfectly builds nothing, and a future lender can’t see it at all. Our own audience asked for this one — TikTok Search, Sep 13: “how to do pay later.”

ZERO

How much of a typical pay-in-four plan reaches your credit file while you’re paying it on time: no tradeline, no utilization, no payment history. A default, though, can still reach collections — and collections do report.

Topic 5 · the through-line resolves here

Is the 401(k) match free money — or a raise your boss took back and locked up until you’re 59½?

Plain English: a match is your employer adding money to your retirement account based on what you put in. The universal framing is “free money, always take it.” The contested framing is that total compensation is one budget — so a match may be pay that got re-routed instead of negotiated, and locked up until 59½.

$1,500 → $1,800

The same 50%-of-6% match on a $50,000 salary versus a $60,000 salary. The formula never changed — the base did. The match is a percentage of a number you negotiate.
