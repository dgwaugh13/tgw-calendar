AI took the office jobs and left the bar jobs. Is a $100,000 degree still worth it?

Plain English: America added a lot of jobs in August — but most of them were at bars, restaurants and schools, while computer-and-media jobs disappeared. If AI is eating the jobs a degree was supposed to buy, is college still the best $100,000 a 20-year-old can spend?

Topic 2 · 13:00–24:00

Diesel just broke $6.00 for the first time in history. Should the government cap fuel prices?

Plain English: diesel is the fuel that moves everything you buy — trucks, trains, tractors. It has never cost $6 a gallon before, and it does now. One side says the government should put a ceiling on it. The other says a ceiling is how you get empty pumps.

$6.05
AAA national average diesel, Friday Sep 11 — the first time in history it has ever started with a six. It was $5.85 one week earlier and $3.70 a year ago: +63.5%. California is at $7.98; Washington and Hawaii are both above $7.
AAA Fuel Prices · Sep 11, 2026 · Fox Business / Newsweek confirm the record
+27.4%
Gasoline over the last 12 months in the CPI — and it rose 3.9% in August alone, accounting for more than a third of the entire monthly increase. AAA gas is $4.313 today.
BLS CPI, August 2026 (rel. Sep 11) · AAA Sep 13
+52.0%
Fuel oil over 12 months (+10.1% in August alone). Heating oil is nearly the same molecule as diesel — which is why winter demand competes with the trucks. Energy overall: +16.3% YoY.
BLS CPI, August 2026
2.0 points
Producer prices rose 5.4% over the year against consumer prices at 3.4%. Businesses are absorbing more than they've passed on — and diesel was named among the leading drivers. That gap is the pipeline.
BLS PPI, August 2026 (rel. Sep 10)
$25K → $45K
What one farm's 10,000-gallon tank now costs to fill. Crop prices are locked months ahead, so the extra is eaten now and shows up in the next contract — a September record becomes a November grocery bill.
NPR, Sep 4, 2026
~1/5 & ~1/3

Topic 3 · 24:00–33:00

Apple wants $1,999 for a phone that folds. Should you EVER finance a phone?

Plain English: Apple's folding phone is here and it costs two thousand dollars. Almost nobody pays that in one go. So the real question isn't whether the phone is worth it — it's whether borrowing for a phone is ever a reasonable thing to do, and what it costs when it goes wrong.

$1,999
iPhone Duo — announced Wednesday Sep 9. Note the name: every rumor called it the "Ultra," and Apple didn't. Pre-orders open Oct 16; it ships Oct 23. Book-style fold, phone when closed, larger display when open.
Apple, Sep 9, 2026 announcement
$83.29
Per month on a true 0% 24-month installment. $1,999 ÷ 24. The total equals the price — that part is real, and it is the cheapest version of this.
Computed Sep 13, 2026
$665.94
Interest if that same $83.29 a month goes on a credit card at 22.15% APR instead: 32 months to clear, total $2,664.94.
Computed Sep 13 · APR per Fed G.19 Q2 2026
8.8 years
On minimum payments only (1% of the balance plus that month's interest, $35 floor) $1,999 takes 106 months and costs $2,191.19 in interest — $4,190.19 total for a $1,999 phone. Say the assumption out loud on air.
Computed Sep 13, 2026 · corrected from the Sep 7 draft
22.15%
Average APR on credit cards that actually carry a balance. Across all accounts it's 20.94%; new offers run near 23.79%. APR in plain English: the price tag on borrowing, per year.
Federal Reserve G.19, Q2 2026
"deferred"
The one word to look for. "No interest for 24 months" is usually deferred interest: it accrues the whole time and gets charged, backdated to day one, if a single dollar is left on month 25. A true installment plan has no interest to defer.

The Fed might raise your rates 7 weeks before an election. Should a central bank ever move in an election window?

Plain English: on Wednesday the Federal Reserve is expected to raise interest rates for the first time in more than three years — which makes borrowing more expensive for everyone — and the midterm elections are seven weeks later. The Fed is designed to ignore the calendar. Should it?

85.5%
Market-implied odds of a 25bp hike on Wednesday (CME FedWatch, Sep 12). It was 60.6% on Sep 8 and 44.4% on Aug 7. Worth saying out loud: Kalshi had it at 57% and Polymarket at 49% the same week — three markets, three answers, and that disagreement is its own lesson.

1,148 days
Since the last Fed rate increase — July 26, 2023. The target range has been 3.50–3.75% after six cuts totalling 175bp. An entire generation of first-time borrowers has never seen this committee go up.
